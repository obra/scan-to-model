# Review visible geometry against sources

Use this process when accepting a new or changed architectural candidate, before
detail and finish work on the affected assembly. Keep the result in the existing
room or model review note; no separate approval or receipt is needed. Scope the
check to the changed room, connection or assembly and its immediate context.

## Make the comparison

1. Select source photos that show the affected extent, openings, levels or
   contacts. Bind their source IDs and the saved candidate identity. A detail
   crop can answer a profile question; it cannot establish an out-of-frame
   opening or the room's overall layout. If no photo shows the question, record
   that gap and use the available scan, plan or native evidence without claiming
   a photo comparison.
2. Where a photo shows the question, render the saved geometry from a camera
   matched to it.
   Match viewpoint, verticals and field of view using fixed, independently
   identifiable scene features where available. Retain the camera settings and
   any alignment limits. Do not fit the camera to make the disputed model edge
   agree. A manually matched pose is diagnostic, not calibrated registration.
   A source-local comparison is still useful when the photo is unregistered,
   provided its perspective uncertainty remains explicit.
3. Put the source photo and geometry render on the same canvas. Inspect an
   adjustable alpha overlay or aligned edge overlay of the relevant boundaries;
   retain the source, render and overlay together. Preserve the camera fit while
   aligning the canvas; do not warp an image to erase a discrepancy. Mark the
   compared features and any regions obscured by furniture, reflections or crop
   boundaries. An apparent offset caused by uncertain pose or lens distortion
   calls for another view or control; it is not automatically a mesh defect. A
   wide-angle tour camera from a different pose is not this comparison.
4. Alongside the overlay, inspect a top view or section of the saved geometry
   that exposes the same question: extent, opening, level and connection for a
   room or stair; local profile and support contact for a fixture. Use native
   inspection when a projected edge or contact is ambiguous. Scale these views
   to the geometry task rather than producing a whole-house drawing for each
   small edit.

## Decide and recheck

In one compact review-note entry, record the source IDs, saved candidate,
matched camera/render and overlay paths, relevant top/section or native view,
the visible cue, and the disposition of each consequential difference:
`geometry conflict`, `camera/source uncertainty`, `supported match`, or
`unresolved`. Say what evidence supports that disposition and which dimensions
or contacts remain tentative. Image hashes, mesh checks and a successful reopen
establish their mechanical contracts, not a visible match.

Correct a supported geometry conflict before spending effort on the affected
details and finishes. Reopen the corrected candidate, remake the affected
source-matched render and overlay, and recheck the relevant top/section and
adjacent connection. If camera or source uncertainty remains, seek an independent
view or control when available; do not stretch the camera or promote a diagnostic
pose match to accepted registration. Under existing interpolation authorization,
retain a reasoned tentative choice when evidence is inconclusive, with its basis
and limit visible in the model and note. A tentative label does not excuse a
known visible contradiction.

After the affected shape is reviewed, compare source and final-size render for
profile, material scale/direction, lighting and other requested appearance.
Reuse accepted comparisons for unchanged geometry and sources. For a complete
house, repeat the bounded check as regions and connections advance, then
reconcile every observed region, level and connection against the saved model
before claiming the whole-house geometry complete.
